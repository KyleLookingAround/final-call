# Version history

Newest first. The version numbers match the published claude.ai artifact.

| Version | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| 32 | **Ready for what's next.** A toast now appears when a new version of Final Call is published while you're playing, with Update now (saves your airport, then reloads to it) and Later (it comes back no sooner than an hour on). A quiet "Buy me a Ko-fi" link sits in What's new, Settings and the level-up card, for anyone who wants to support the game; it never appears on the airport view, the board or the guided start. Help has a Send feedback link, shown only on the published site, that opens a new GitHub issue with your version, level, layout, game day and screen size already filled in. The game now saves on this device only, since it's published only on GitHub Pages; save codes still move an airport between devices, and older saves keep loading. |
| 31 | **Looks like a real airport.** The airport view is drawn as a real one. Planes have engines under the wings with the airline's colour on their cowls and fin, a soft shadow on the apron, and at night wingtip and tail lights, a flashing beacon while towed in or pushed back, and strobes on the runway. Each turnaround has a fuel truck and a catering truck, a baggage tractor with carts while bags move, and a tug for the pushback. Built stands get painted lead-in lines, stop bars and safety lines with their numbers, taxi lines have centre lines, and runways have their stripes, thresholds and numbers. After dark, floodlight masts, runway and taxiway edge lights and lit terminal windows come on, and the airfield turns blue before dawn, warm at sunrise and sunset, and blue again at dusk. The Roof button on the map's camera bar steps up to the roof, over every built hall with its skylights and plant, and back down to the halls, at any zoom; the halls are never covered otherwise. Rain leaves puddles that shine under the lights, snow settles on the apron and grass but is cleared from the stands, fog banks roll in, cloud shadows drift over faster as a storm nears, and a windsock stands by the runway. Nothing about how the game plays has changed. |
| 30 | **A card for each new level.** Reaching a level opens a card naming it, with the cash and plan points it brings and only what it has just unlocked: new gates and Pier B, the upgrades that can go higher, the new plans in the Masterplan with the aircraft, routes and cities, and transport each brings, and anything that opens up (the Region and the World map, weekly challenges, Lowmere, consultants). Each line links straight there, and following one closes the card. The game waits while it's open and carries on at the same speed after. Levels reached together share one card. It never shows during the guided start, and Office › Settings › Notifications turns it off. |
| 29 | **The airport sounds like one.** The terminal announces your own flights (not partners' or freighters'): boarding, gate calls from the market place, final calls when the board first shows FINAL CALL, and gate changes. Each chimes and shows its words along the foot of the departures board, scrolling on a narrow phone. Now and then, at 1× or 2×, a British voice reads a final call or a gate change, at most one every five minutes. The terminal's hum, jets taking off and landing (panned by where the runway is on screen) and rain follow the camera: zoom into the halls and the hum rises while the rain fades. From 23:00 to 05:00 the hum drops and only final calls chime. Office › Settings › Sound turns announcements (on, chime only, off), spoken calls, ambience and effects on or off, under the master switch. |
| 28 | **A real terminal.** Passengers go through halls in the order real airports use them. Departing: four check-in islands, kiosks and bag drop, then a boarding-pass gate and a security hall where lanes divest, scan and repack, with bags searched at tables (CT scanners search fewer). Arriving: an immigration hall with passport desks and e-gates in their own queues, a domestic channel, customs with a red channel, and an arrivals hall with meeters and a taxi rank. A baggage hall screens and sorts bags onto tug trains to the plane; a flight leaves late bags behind for a courier, and tight transfers can miss. Arriving bags share carousels, and the board says ON BELT or BAGS LATE. The market place fills with passengers until the board calls their gate: shops have room for so many, with tills and tables; the Gate calls policy or the duty manager sets when gates are called, and later calls mean more shopping; gate lounges have seats; a walk-through duty free can be built. An airport hotel takes late arrivals, early flyers, connections, delegates, crews and stranded passengers. The advisor points at a full café or hotel, search tables have staff wages, and the simulation runs about twice as fast. |
| 27 | **A smarter transport manager.** The manager runs your region lines by what each change is worth to the network and the airport: how often they run, fares and meeting flights. It suggests upgrading a line to a tram, train, metro or high-speed rail, extending one by a station, or closing one other lines now do better, each with its value, payback and a preview on the map. Any line can be upgraded from its card, and keeps running until the new one opens. |

## Design rules that have held throughout

- No choice of scenario at the start; everyone starts at the same airfield.
- Locked things are hidden, not greyed out.
- Changes show on the map, board and gates, not as text events.
- Text is concise.
- Balance is checked with the bot over about 1,150 game hours, and the headless run must keep working.
