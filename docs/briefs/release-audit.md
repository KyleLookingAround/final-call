# Brief: Release audit, what stands between Final Call and a polished public release

The owner, 28 Sep: "keep going till you have the two floors and then do a final refactor sweep, any fixes or balance fixes sweep, any easy wins, basically the game actual release at that point. Make it very polished, you can change anything to get to that point. I'll wait for you to finish before I start sharing with the wider public. Current feedback is that it 'really scratched a brain itch' so that's the aim."

This session finds the work. It fixes nothing. The coordinator turns its findings into fix sessions once two floors in Classic merges.

## Goal and what it may touch

- Write `docs/ideas/release-audit.md`, one PR on `feature/release-audit` from `main`, docs only, plus one GitHub issue per batch, labelled `release`. Find what to do, by playing, measuring and reading, in five parts:
  1. **The brain itch.** Players say the game scratches a brain itch, so what feeds that and what gets in its way? Play a new game for its first two hours at 4×, then the level 3, 5 and 9 saves (`tools/saves/`), and ask these questions:
     - Is there always a clear next decision?
     - Does a choice pay off visibly on the map within a minute or two?
     - Are there dead stretches with nothing to decide, or walls where the player is stuck?
     - Does anything feel like busywork a manager should take?
     - Does the late game keep asking something new?
     Name each moment, where it happens and its fix.
  2. **Bugs.** Run `npm run check` and the bot on seeds 1–3, and read the Health check workflow's last runs. Look for anything wrong you can see or reproduce: console errors, stuck passengers or planes, numbers that jump or go negative, saves that don't round-trip, a setting that does nothing.
  3. **Balance.** Compare the bot on seeds 1–3 against `tools/baseline.json` and the `balance` playbook. Which levels drag or rush, which upgrades or policies nobody should ever take or always must, and whether money snowballs or starves. Propose the changes and the checks that would prove them.
  4. **Refactors.** Which of `docs/specs/systems-review.md` steps 8–10, or anything else, is worth doing before release because it lowers the risk of a player-visible bug? Which isn't, and why? Refactor 7 is running now; leave it out.
  5. **Easy wins and polish.** Small things with a big effect on feel: sound or motion at the moments that matter, clearer numbers, the first minute, text that could be shorter, anything at 320 px. Don't repeat rows `docs/ideas/polish-launch.md` and `polish-2026-09.md` already fixed.
- **Leave out the terminal's halls and floors.** Two floors in Classic is being built now, and a polish pass over the terminal follows it. Note terminal issues in a separate short list for that pass.
- **Rank every row** by how much a player notices it against how small the fix is. Each row gives where it is, what's wrong, the fix in a sentence, its size, the files it would touch, and how to prove it (a check, a bot run, a screenshot).
- **Group the rows into batches** of 3–6, with **no two batches sharing a file**. Mark each batch refactor, fix, balance or polish, and give an order: refactors first in a quiet window, then fixes and balance, then polish. Mark which rows are for the owner rather than a fix session: anything beyond the balance baselines' tolerance, or a spec-sized change.
- It may touch: `docs/ideas/release-audit.md`, one roadmap item in `docs/roadmap.d/`, its look back in `docs/lessons/`, and this brief as `docs/briefs/release-audit.md`. Scripts and screenshots stay in `build/`. No game code, no checks.
