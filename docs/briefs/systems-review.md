# Brief: Systems review, for code and game design

## Goal and what it may touch

- The owner wants the game's systems to work smoothly together and interact in meaningful ways, so that choices are interesting. They are open to refactoring the code and reworking systems to get there. This session reads the whole game and writes a plan: **no game code**. Branch `feature/systems-review` from `main`, one PR.
- **Deliver `docs/specs/systems-review.md`** (status Proposed), with three parts:
  1. **How the systems connect today.** Take the airport, flights and airlines, passengers, the terminal, the region and transport, routes and the world, Lowmere and rivals, managers, levels and the Masterplan, money, events, and records and challenges. For each, say:
     - what feeds it and what it feeds;
     - which of the player's choices it responds to;
     - where it runs on its own, where one choice always wins, and where a system can be ignored with no cost.

     Draw it as one diagram (Mermaid in the doc is fine) and back it with evidence:
     - the code (`src/game/<file>:<line>`, `node tools/graph.mjs`);
     - bot runs on `main` (seeds 1–3, side by side in the background; `docs/ideas/game-logic.md` already has findings to build on, such as the rating stuck at 91–100, every fleet ending as 21 widebodies, and cash piling up);
     - a real play of `build/test.html`.
  2. **Design: making choices interesting.** Eight to twelve proposals that link systems so a choice in one shows up in others. Each gets:
     - the trade-off the player faces;
     - what they'd see (the owner prefers impacts on the map, board and gates over text);
     - which systems it ties together;
     - how managers and recommendations handle it for players who don't want the details;
     - the risk to the balance baselines and old saves;
     - a size.

     Build on `docs/ideas/game-logic.md`, the idea board (`docs/ideas/board-2026-09.md` once rated) and the owner's bundles in `docs/ROADMAP.md` (4 "Passengers with a voice", 5 "A 24-hour airport with stakes", 6 "Hub and a second airport"), rather than repeating them. Say which bundle each proposal belongs in, or whether it should come before them.
  3. **Code: refactors that make that possible.** Ranked steps, each small enough for one session and PR, each keeping `PLAY` identical on seeds 1–3 (a pure refactor), with the checks to write first. Candidates to look at:
     - functions far from their system (`pickRoute` in `03-state.js`);
     - several passes over passengers per step (the roadmap's "Speed on throttled phones");
     - shared loops that features keep editing, where a hook table like the terminal's `PAX_STEP`/`TERM_MINUTE` would do;
     - one place for the effects between systems, so the design proposals don't scatter cross-calls;
     - `12-drawing.js` after the real-airport parts;
     - `SIMX` getters copied at load (`__sim.AF_Y` always reads 0, found by the planes part);
     - save-migration risk in `resetAll`.

     For each step, say what it unblocks in part 2, and where it fits around the work already planned: the real airport's bring-together PR, then "The terminal as a place" (spec approved, #48). Could a step be its groundwork?
- **End with a recommended order** across all three parts, and a `needs-owner` issue asking the owner to approve the plan. The default after 12 hours is to leave it Proposed: nothing gets built from it without approval.
- Files it may touch: `docs/specs/systems-review.md`, one line in `docs/ROADMAP.md` pointing to it, this brief as `docs/briefs/systems-review.md`, and a look back in `docs/LESSONS.md`. Anything else is outside the brief.
